Interventional endocrinology / illustrative cases

Adrenal venous sampling (AVS)

In patients with primary aldosteronism considering surgery, AVS helps distinguish lateralizing from bilateral aldosterone secretion. Click sampling targets in the 3D vascular view. The right adrenal vein is short and usually enters the IVC directly; the left adrenal vein commonly joins the inferior phrenic vein before entering the left renal vein. Variants are common.

Synthetic examples / no patient data

Example SI rule: >5 stimulated; >2 unstimulated (center dependent).
2025 Endocrine Society guideline (source 13) describes protocol-dependent selectivity cutoffs and LI ≥4 supporting lateralization; LI <3 favors bilateral secretion, 3–4 is indeterminate. Cortisol cosecretion, sampling errors and assay issues can confound interpretation; this is not clinical AVS interpretation or a procedural navigation tool.
Evidence & limitations

Research behind the atlas

Classical adult-human zonation is a teaching scaffold, not a strict boundary. Human spatial and single-cell studies show patchy CYP11B2-positive foci, transitional states, stromal, immune and neuroendocrine populations, and age-associated heterogeneity. Geometry, cell counts, cell coordinates, marker placement, vessel caliber, histology and all perturbation amplitudes here are artistically generated, not measured tissue or patient imaging. The 3D section is not to scale; the disease overlays are exemplars, not diagnostic scans. No patient information is used. No internet connection is required to operate the atlas; reference links open externally.
