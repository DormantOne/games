Clinical background and limitations

The hypotheses use conventional regularity, rate and atrial association concepts, but the weights are experimental, not guideline-derived. A broad excursion is not a measured QRS duration. Regular wide-QRS tachycardia can reflect VT or supraventricular mechanisms; a 12-lead ECG and clinical context are necessary. See 2019 ESC SVT guideline. This research tool does not advise treatment. A potentially dangerous ongoing rhythm requires prompt clinical assessment.

Methods, limitations and privacy
Processing runs locally in this browser. No network calls or third-party libraries. Uploaded images are not persisted. Auto baseline is a robust straight-line fit to the densest trace level across columns; it can mistake gridlines, fibrillatory activity or a sustained deflection for baseline. Parallel lines are in image coordinates; substantial perspective distortion or baseline wander needs manual correction or a better scan. Dark grid, text, pacer spikes, clipping and overlapping leads can contaminate results. No clinical QRS onset/offset, QT or definitive P-wave identification is attempted. Ranked rhythm hypotheses are exploratory. Rate is a candidate rate only, and only shown with horizontal calibration and paper speed. Use original tracing for all clinical decisions.

FIELD GUIDE / V5
Start here: a five-minute experiment
  1. Load a demo. Start with regular; then choose irregular or alternating morphology. Click Load demo. Known synthetic calibration is supplied.
  2. Inspect the detected peaks. Compare markers to the source trace; an anchor is not necessarily a QRS. Check missed or doubled events.
  3. Upload a de-identified single lead. Under advanced settings crop the strip; remove labels, calibration pulses and borders. Calibrate X and Y by selecting two grid points a known distance apart; verify paper speed independently.
  4. Check the baseline and ink. Toggle mask; adjust threshold and use two isoelectric points only if genuinely identifiable. Broad continuous waveforms may not have an obvious baseline.
  5. Read the ranked hypotheses. Scores are relative heuristic support, not probabilities. Open all formulas and inspect the contributing features. A tied VT and SVT score means unresolved mechanism.
  6. Explore shape. Click sweep rows, then an apex branch. Compare width growth, curvature, asymmetry and the median beat/residual plot. A shoulder is not automatically atrial activity.
  7. Freeze a case in LLM handoff. Choose blind or assisted. Copy the image or download PNG; ask for the exact JSON. Import the return into the same case.
  8. Review and learn. In Review & accuracy choose yes/no/uncertain for each feature and optionally record overall agreement. Export the ledger. No human reference = no accuracy estimate.
What can and cannot be inferred
Use the clickable i buttons on every major section. Geometry is repeatable mathematical evidence, not a clinical diagnosis. Excursion widths are not QRS duration; candidate offsets are not PR/QT. V6 estimates separate single-lead candidate boundaries; verify all markings. QT is QRS onset to T end; correction depends on preceding RR and is withheld for broad or markedly irregular candidates. Absent detected P-like peaks do not prove absent P waves. Single-lead VT/SVT discrimination is often impossible. No detection of a dangerous rhythm is a safety guarantee. The tool does not assess pulselessness, ventricular fibrillation or asystole reliably. Seek immediate clinical assessment for an ongoing concerning rhythm; do not use this lab to guide treatment.
