HOW THE BRAIN LEARNS

At every junction, each train tests legal exits through its team’s 24-input, 9-neuron recurrent policy, combining local food, enemy size, tail position, HP, vision, junction type and recent visits with its own persistent memory. Outputs score direction, speed and division.
After a round, the better-scoring squad’s complete weights are retained. A mutated copy challenges it on a new random track. All three starting agents and their offspring contribute to team fitness. This is evolutionary self-play, not backpropagation or a pretrained CNN.
CNNs excel on pixels; here the agents read a compact graph of track connections. A recurrent network gives each locomotive its own working memory without six separate brains.
