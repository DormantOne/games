Read this first: fitness was exploitable, and 1900 generations found the exploit. Corpses inside a chamber used to be recycled into the larder, and fitness counted larder and population. An ant that never walks burns almost nothing, dies at home, and funds its replacement — a near-lossless loop that pays without foraging. The archive told the story plainly: the top-scoring genomes (fitness 399, 379, 376) all had deliver 0.00 and a range of 0.06–0.41, while entries from generations 4–24 that actually delivered scored 231–311. The scent genome showed the strategy directly: two colonies had inverted their own footprint channel to 1.28 and 2.10 at high concentration — attractive when thick, so their own tracks became a reason to stay put. The one colony that kept the band-pass intact (−0.75) was the only one still foraging, and had the lowest fitness of the four. Both holes are now shut: the dead are carried out to a midden so their material returns to the world, and fitness counts income only — food carried in, part credit for finding it, and combat. A colony physically unable to walk now scores 0.2 against 83.7. If you are resuming an old run, press Restart lineage: an archive built under the old fitness is full of genomes that farm their own dead.

Episode length matters more than generation count. At equal compute, twelve 900-tick generations lifted champions 12% but lifted the random-drift control 14% too — over a short window a coin stumbles into looking competent. Six 1800-tick generations lifted champions 20% while drift collapsed 55%. The founding wave needs ~1500 ticks to settle to the sustainable population, and recruitment trails last 900–1800 ticks, so a short window cannot watch a trail pay off at all. Default is 1800 with a 45% burn-in. Because champion and challenger run the identical maze the comparison has no measurement noise, so the remaining noise is generalisation — that is what mazes per trial is for, and with income-only fitness (a small-count statistic) 2–3 is worth the cost.

256 liquid neurons under Dale's law, 204 excitatory and 44 inhibitory, plus 8 tonic pacemakers that ignore their senses and oscillate at an evolvable tempo — runs have produced periods from 12 to 667 ticks in the same brain. Micro-Hebbian mutation: active pathways mutate 4.6× more often in 1.6× smaller steps, quiet ones rarely but boldly. A diversity archive keeps champions for being different on a four-dimensional behaviour descriptor, and reseeds the lineage when the record stalls for twelve generations.

What selection found before it went astray. All four colonies independently pushed their recruitment channel to 865–1800 ticks while holding footprints short — the persistence/density relationship that took a hand-run parameter sweep to discover. Three of four then pinned the old ceiling of 1800 exactly, which made it a clamp artefact rather than an optimum, so the ceiling is raised: trails may now persist for thousands of ticks. Rival readings became opinions rather than defaults, with colonies following one neighbour's trails while fleeing another's, and disagreeing about each other.

Underneath: food conserved to a fixed total and circulating; three scent channels whose meaning, valence and fade rate are genomic; recruitment where picking food up marks the spot; homing by nest odour; instinct on a dial from blank slate to fully wired. Biopsy dumps the archive, pacemaker periods, evolved scent curves and the champion-versus-drift gap — and the archive is the first place to look when a number seems too good.
