1. The anatomy and the rule

LAFB: posterior fascicle conducts first; delayed forces go superiorly and leftward. Look for −45° to −90°, qR in aVL, rS inferiorly, and QRS <120 ms.

LPFB: anterior fascicle conducts first; delayed forces go inferiorly and rightward. Look for +90° to +180°, rS in I/aVL, qR inferiorly, QRS <120 ms, and exclude other right-axis causes.

Axis alone never proves a fascicular block. RV hypertrophy, emphysema, infarction, congenital variation and lead misplacement can mimic the axis.

3. Measure QRS and estimate axis

Use the largest representative beat. Count millimeters above and below baseline. Net QRS = R − |Q| − |S|. For an R-only complex, Q and S are zero.

Waiting for measurements.

Calculation: lead I = X and aVF = Y (orthogonal frontal projections); angle = atan2(Y,X). Lead II is an independent consistency check. This is an estimate from manually measured net amplitudes, not an automated ECG interpretation.
