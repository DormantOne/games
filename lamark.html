How it works · motor, sensory, and the comparison click to expand

One physical task: A four-link inverted pendulum starts tilted, responds to four scheduled pushes, and tries to become and stay upright. Four limited joint motors move its rods. Both methods receive the same 16 sensory channels: four absolute angles (sine), four angular velocities, and four angle measurements (base plus adjacent-joint differences) and four velocity measurements (base plus adjacent-joint differences).

The brain: 8–256 simultaneously updated liquid-timescale-inspired neurons in an interconnected circle. Neighboring cells and cross-ring links recur in both directions around the network, not a chain of isolated feedforward layers. Each neuron can read sensory inputs, while four readouts drive the motors.

Motor + sensory: From one identical simulated physical state, the learner tries paired positive and negative, coordinated four-motor torque sequences over several steps. Each trial rolls the whole body forward and scores its result. It records the changing sensory readings and internal neural activations, uses the measured motor advantage to propose small changes to readout and recurrent/sensory weights, and tests six update strengths. It preserves a candidate only if its full-episode selection fitness improves. Sensory readings are observed, not artificially changed.

Straight evolution: The other arm randomly mutates weights, biases, and liquid time-constant parameters, tests full episodes, and retains only better candidate networks. Its mutation density scales with neuron count by default.

Fair comparison: Same initialized network at each size and seed, identical disturbance and selection seeds, matched simulated physics transitions for each arm, with motor probes and failed candidates counted. Independent final-test episodes are only evaluated once training ends. The experiment additionally reports a neural multiply-add proxy and approximate wall time, because matching physics does not mean matching computation at different cell counts.

Long runs: The budget is entered in thousands of physics transitions per method, neuron size, and seed. More budget allows more completed learning generations; generations have no separate small fixed cap. At large sizes, many seeds or multi-million-step runs can take a long time in a browser. Keep the tab open and use Pause/Stop as needed.

Scientific caution: Reflex-assisted results measure the contribution of the learned controller on top of the same baseline reflex, not unaided balancing. A larger controller also has more parameters and a different circular graph; scaling here is a benchmark observation, not a universal law.

Interpretation & limitations

Both arms share four-link physics, four motor limits, 16 sensory channels, same initial weights at the same neuron count/seed, and identical selection/final test disturbances. Motor-first updates output, sensory and recurrent weights from several time steps of signed motor perturbations. Evolution mutates the same weight groups and state-dependent liquid time-constant parameters. By default its mutation count scales with network size, avoiding an automatic reduction in the fraction of weights touched at larger neuron counts; a fixed-count alternative is available as a separate diagnostic; motor-first does not directly update time constants. Matching physics transitions does not match CPU or neuron operations as neuron count grows: approximate forward multiply-adds and rough milliseconds are recorded separately. The 56% default reflex is an external stabilizer—not evidence the liquid brain balances alone. A change in neuron count also changes the circular graph and parameter count. This is an experimental simulation, not an established scaling law.

Physical model and learning rules

Four serial uniform rods, full coupled four-body dynamics, distributed inertia, gravity, damping and four limited relative-joint actuators. Starting near ±65° with four reproducible scheduled pushes. Paired motor perturbation sequences span 2–8 steps, with a 24-step lookahead. Motor and sensor histories are used for approximate backward eligibility credit; there is no full physics backpropagation or full backpropagation through time. For each motor proposal, six conservative update strengths are evaluated on fixed selection episodes before replacing the champion. Evolution evaluates five sparse mutations per generation, including the state-dependent liquid time-constant parameters. Entire unfinished generations are discarded when the physical-step cap is reached.
