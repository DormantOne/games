How the objective works

Candidate thresholds divide the grayscale histogram into 2–9 bands. Four-neighbor connectivity defines components of a single band. Islands smaller than the selected area floor remain visible as muted pixels but are not counted. Optional smoothing acts before thresholding. The search favors explained intensity variance, boundaries with strong original-image contrast, fewer fragmented boundaries and less small-component area, then penalizes distance from the target. Colors are assigned after selection. Global thresholding cannot distinguish overlapping tissues with the same intensity, and forcing a count can create artificial partitions. Results are not a proof of optimality or a clinical finding.
