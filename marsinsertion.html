Mission guide & model fidelity

Fly the mission

  1. Begin the slow 12-second launch and wait for trans-Mars injection.
  2. During cruise, use 0.25–1 m/s impulses to keep the predicted periapsis close to the 400 km target. The default trajectory is already inside the safe corridor.
  3. At Mars approach, arm the insertion burn. The simulator executes the retrograde burn at detected periapsis.
  4. Once a stable orbit is reported, deploy the probe. Heat shield, parachute, and powered descent sequence automatically.

Physics model

The transfer uses SI-compatible kilometers and seconds, NASA reference radii, semimajor axes, periods, and standard gravitational parameters. Spacecraft propagation uses fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration under solar gravity, adding Mars gravity inside its sphere of influence. The departure is a patched-conic, minimum-energy Hohmann transfer with Mars initially ≈44.34° ahead and a nominal flight time of ≈258.88 days. Course controls apply true impulsive Δv in the local radial, along-track, and orbit-normal frame.

For clarity, planetary radii and the spacecraft are visually exaggerated in the system view. Planet orbits are simplified as circular and coplanar. Mars insertion circularizes at detected periapsis. Atmospheric entry uses an exponential Mars atmosphere, drag by ballistic coefficient, parachute deployment logic, and closed-loop terminal descent. It is educational—not operational flight software.
