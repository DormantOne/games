the idea

Why a frequency dial?

A normal keyboard scatters the letters you use most all over the place. This dial does the opposite — it's a heat-map of the alphabet. The hottest letters in English sit in the inner ring, where your thumb rests; rarer letters cool off toward the edge. Because the layout never changes, your thumb learns the map by feel, and short common words like names turn into muscle memory. Speed comes from not deciding, so everything here is tuned to strip out hesitation.

You can drive it with one thumb, or split it for two thumbs — each thumb gets its own half of the alphabet, its own backspace, and its own ring, so neither ever crosses the middle. There's also a plain keyboard so you can race the dial against what your thumbs already know; the Progress tab tracks your letters-per-second on each. As you type, the keys glow toward the letters most likely to come next in English (common letter pairs) — a nudge for speed that never gives away the name. A three-finger split is possible in theory, but on the web a page can't watch the camera to track a third finger reliably, so that one waits for a native app.
