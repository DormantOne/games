How to drive it & what you're seeing

Goal: float the balloon-tipped catheter from the vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery, then obtain a wedge (PCWP) tracing. Watch each waveform change as the tip crosses a valve, and respond to alarms.

Technique that keeps the patient safe: advance to the RA, inflate the balloon, then advance — the balloon floats the tip across the tricuspid and pulmonary valves and reduces ventricular irritation. Deflate before withdrawing. Don't linger in the RV, and don't over-advance once wedged.

Transitions to recognize: RA→RV the systolic pressure jumps; RV→PA the diastolic pressure rises and a dicrotic notch appears; PA→wedge the trace loses pulsatility and damps to a low atrial pattern.

Alarms model real complications: ventricular ectopy / VT from a catheter loitering in the RV, over-wedge (pulmonary-artery rupture risk) from advancing too far with the balloon up, and spontaneous wedge / infarction risk from a tip that has migrated too distal.
