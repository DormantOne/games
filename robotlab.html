Build the real tabletop arm

A buildable 4-axis teaching rig: base yaw → shoulder pitch → elbow pitch → wrist pitch → parallel-jaw gripper, plus a fixed overhead camera, a wrist camera, and an optional ultrasonic range sensor. It can gently capture a foam ball and release it during a controlled swing. Treat the throw as a physics demonstration, never a weapon.

Joint limits: yaw ±165°, shoulder 5–150°, elbow ±145°, wrist ±105°. Enter your measured link lengths into firmware so the inverse kinematics match the hardware.

Electronics & wiring

  1. One microcontroller runs high-level commands and the JSON interpreter.
  2. A PWM/servo driver or smart-servo bus drives the joints — never power servos from the microcontroller's 5 V pin.
  3. Use a separate high-current servo supply (6–12 V depending on servo class) with an inline fuse. Tie all grounds together: supply, driver, and microcontroller.
  4. Mount Camera A above/in front of the workspace; mount Camera B on the wrist looking along the gripper. USB-UVC cameras work with OpenCV / AprilTags / colour-blob tracking.
  5. Wire an emergency stop that cuts servo power. Add software joint limits and mechanical hard stops.

Calibration sequence

  1. Servo power OFF, place the arm in a safe home pose (shoulder up, elbow slightly bent, gripper above the table).
  2. Power only the controller; confirm each joint reports a sane neutral.
  3. Move one joint at a time at low speed; verify the positive direction of yaw, shoulder, elbow, wrist.
  4. Measure exact link lengths; enter them into the interpreter.
  5. Set soft limits so no link or jaw can reach below the table plane.
  6. Close the jaws on the ball by hand; tune foam-pad spacing so it holds by gentle compression, not crushing.
  7. Test slow pick-ups first. Only after repeatable gripping should you try a gentle release swing.

Command schema

{
  "name": "pick and place",
  "units": "meters/degrees/seconds",
  "commands": [
    {"cmd":"ball", "x":0.24, "y":0.10, "z":0.04, "mass":0.09},
    {"cmd":"goto", "x":0.24, "y":0.10, "z":0.12, "wrist":-35, "duration":1.0},
    {"cmd":"grip", "state":"close", "duration":0.3},
    {"cmd":"wait", "duration":0.2},
    {"cmd":"goto", "x":0.18, "y":0.05, "z":0.30, "wrist":0,  "duration":0.8},
    {"cmd":"goto", "x":0.37, "y":-0.03,"z":0.24, "wrist":12, "duration":0.35},
    {"cmd":"grip", "state":"open",  "duration":0.08}
  ]
}

Also supported: move (direct joint angles), wait, and settorque (per-joint Nm limits, to model stronger actuators).

Safety. Foam ball, low speeds, eye protection, a clear shield, and keep humans out of the swing plane. If a torque bar in the telemetry goes red, the real machine needs shorter links, lighter payload, gear reduction, a counterbalance spring, or stronger actuators before you build it.
