    Anatomy of the payload

    The wrapper assembles its blocks in the same order every time. The rules come before the case, so the model reads the case already knowing how its answer will be judged.

    1. Header. The 2025 ACC reference, the chosen depth, how many lenses and documents are included, and an education-only banner.
    2. Reference data. The handle to the private key: Shock Bot team, General channel, Files, shockjson.docx.
    3. Analyst role and rules. Infer details from the case. Skip generic openers. Cite case data or a specific guideline figure. Say what is missing and why it matters. Cross-reference documents and note discrepancies. No treatment recommendations.
    4. Clinical emphasis. SCAI staging, etiology categories and write-ins, device and vasoactive focus, any hemodynamic values, and the flagged inflection point.
    5. Case data. Every pasted document, under its own label.
    6. Audience. Fellow, attending, shock team, M&M or QI, transfer team, or general, each with its own writing instruction.
    7. Specific focus. Your priority question and context, if you gave one.
    8. Analysis tasks. One block of numbered questions for each lens you selected.
    9. Synthesis. A case summary, the key decision points, a concordance score out of 10 with its justification, the primary learning point, and the questions still unanswered.
    10. Web search instruction. Added only when the evidence lens is on.

    Small details that do a lot

    The token meter turns amber as you approach the budget and red past it. The page warns you before you leave a pasted document unsaved. A short spoken guide explains each step. None of it needs a server.

    Lens, key, or rule?

    If it is…It belongs in…For example
    A question you ask of every caseA lensWas escalation timely, or delayed?
    A definition, code, or list everyone should shareThe keyValid SCAI stages, device names, etiology codes
    A rule about how to answerThe analyst rulesCite a specific figure for every guideline claim

    Keeping the three apart is what makes the system easy to maintain. A new device goes in the key. A new question goes in a lens. Neither change touches the other.

    Make the key prove it was read

    Put a version line at the top of the key, for example "schema_version": "1.4", along with the date it was updated. Then add one sentence to the payload’s rules: state the schema version you used. If an answer can’t name the version, the key wasn’t read, and the analysis didn’t use your vocabulary.

    Why a Word file in a Teams channel

    Copilot reads Word documents directly, and a file in a channel’s Files tab is stored in the team’s SharePoint site, so it carries the team’s permissions. For a standard channel like General, membership in the team is the access list.

    Exchanging the key

    1. Edit shockjson in place and raise its version number.
    2. Note what changed, in one line, at the top of the file.
    3. Rerun a few known cases to confirm nothing broke.
    4. Every payload uses the new key the next time it runs. The wrapper doesn’t change.

    Another program swaps in its own key by changing one line in the wrapper: the handle in the reference data block.

    One request, step by step

    1. You paste the payload and send it.
    2. Copilot’s orchestrator reads the prompt and plans what to retrieve.
    3. Grounding: with a Microsoft 365 Copilot license and work data on, Copilot searches Microsoft Graph, helped by the semantic index. Copilot Chat without that license is grounded in the web, so attach the file yourself.
    4. Permission trimming: only content you can already open is eligible.
    5. The retrieved passages join your payload in the model’s working context.
    6. The model writes the analysis. Think Deeper sends the work to a reasoning model.
    7. Post-processing: further grounding calls, responsible AI checks, and security and compliance tasks.
    8. The answer comes back with references to what it used.

    Habits that make it reliable

    Type / in the prompt box and choose shockjson.docx to attach it directly. Check every answer’s references for the key. Sign in with your work account so enterprise data protection applies, and follow your institution’s policy on what patient information may be used.

    Microsoft notes that the Copilot web address is moving from m365.cloud.microsoft to copilot.cloud.microsoft. That’s worth knowing if the wrapper’s Open Copilot link ever stops working.

    What changes with Think Deeper

    A reasoning model spends more computation before it answers: it plans, works through intermediate steps, and checks them. It’s slower, and for a long, multi-document case it is usually more thorough.

    CPO = MAP × CO ÷ 451. In the SHOCK trial registry, lower cardiac power tracked with higher in-hospital mortality, and 0.53 W is a commonly cited cutoff from that work. Educational demonstration only.

    Why kappa

    Two reviewers will agree some of the time by chance alone. Cohen’s kappa corrects raw agreement for that, which makes it a fairer test of whether the Shock Bot and your experts really see the same stage.

    Where the field is pointing

    TRIPOD-LLM (Nature Medicine, 2025) sets out what studies of language models should report, with emphasis on transparency, human oversight, and task-specific performance. The Joint Commission and Coalition for Health AI guidance from 2025 asks health systems for AI governance, local validation, and ongoing, risk-based monitoring.
