Cosine similarity Cosine measures direction, not length. Imagine two arrows starting at the same point. The cosine of the angle between them is +1 for parallel arrows, 0 at a right angle, and −1 for opposite arrows. Divide their dot product by both lengths to remove the effect of magnitude. In embeddings it can indicate relatedness, but does not guarantee meaning or truth.

Cosine from first principles

For A = (1, 0, 0) and B = (1, 1, 0), the dot product is 1, their lengths are 1 and √2, and the cosine is 1/√2 ≈ 0.707. The angle is 45°. Multiplying B by 10 leaves its angle and cosine unchanged.

Real models often use hundreds or thousands of dimensions. The same formula applies, although we cannot draw the full space in 3D. The 2D view here is a projection, not a new calculation.
